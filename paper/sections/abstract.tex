Retrieval over PDFs depends on how documents are parsed and cut into
chunks, and recent work proposes choosing the chunking method per
document. We test this in a pre-registered study with two parsers, a pool
of chunkers including a structure-tree method of our own, hybrid
retrieval, and one frozen scorer that reads retrieved chunks up to a fixed
token budget. On \AccountingMain{} held-out documents, opened only after
the analysis plan was frozen, a split-half per-document oracle does not
beat the best single setup (gap \RQonePrimaryGap{} \RQonePrimaryGapCI{});
by the pre-registered rule the answer is \RQoneDecision{}, and one fixed
setup is the recommendation. At an equal token budget smaller chunks
retrieve more of the gold, while the usual fixed-$k$ measures favour
large chunks. A popular library's default chunk size loses
\RQfiveBLangchainRecursiveDoclingDiff{} recall on Docling to a tuned size, whereas
tuning parser settings or our own chunkers' sizes gives no significant
gain. Answers from a language model confirm the penalty for large chunks
but do not separate small-chunk setups. We report every deviation from
the plan and release the result files.
