\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Study design and pre-registration}
\label{sec:prereg}

Figure~\ref{fig:overview} summarises the pipeline. Each PDF is parsed by
one of two parsers, split into chunks by one chunker, indexed for hybrid
retrieval, and scored by a single frozen scorer. We call one combination
of parser, chunker and chunker setting a \emph{setup}.

\input{sections/overview_figure}

The study design was written before the experiments and versioned. The
first specification was frozen on \DateSpecVone{};
the chunker pool and the oracle analysis were added on
\DateSpecVoneOne{}, before any retrieval result existed;
and the held-out sets were specified on \DateSpecVoneTwo{},
before any of their documents was opened. The scorer
(\texttt{scorer\_v1}) was frozen and tagged on \DateScorerFrozen{},
and the held-out plan, including the decision rule for RQ1, was frozen
and pushed to a public repository the same day. The held-out set was
first scored on \DateHeldOutScored{}, for RQ1; its later uses are listed in
\S\ref{sec:deviations}.
Each frozen version is a tagged commit with a public push time
(Appendix~\ref{app:timeline}). Every later change is reported as a
deviation (\S\ref{sec:deviations}; Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}).

\subsection{Documents and questions}
\label{sec:data}

We use digital PDFs from five public question-answering collections:
contracts from CUAD v1 \citep{hendrycks2021cuad}, scientific papers from
QASPER \citep{dasigi2021qasper}, OHR-Bench v2
\citep{zhang2024ocr}, UniDoc-Bench \citep{peng2025unidoc} and
FinanceBench \citep{islam2023financebench}. We add Indian regulatory
documents with the questions of \citet{singh2026parser} and a small Hindi
track of our own. Scanned PDFs, used only for RQ5, come from the
MP-DocVQA validation split \citep{tito2023mpdocvqa}.

The \AccountingHeldOut{} held-out documents were chosen by a fixed rule
written before any of them was opened. A \emph{type} is a source
collection, split by domain where the collection has domain labels
(OHR-Bench, UniDoc-Bench). For each type in the tuning set we took the
largest unseen document by that collection's own length metadata; an
out-of-domain group applies the same rule to unused domains, and adds one
FinanceBench document from the top decile by question count. Gold
answers are character spans that we keep only when they are certain
(per-collection rules in Appendix~\ref{app:gold}).

Of the \TuningDocuments{} tuning documents,
\TuningScoredDocuments{} have at least one usable question;
the UniDoc-Bench documents have only page-level gold, and two QASPER
documents have no gold span that can be placed with certainty. QASPER
is reported separately (the rationale was not recorded), so
the main tuning analysis uses \TuningMainDocuments{} documents and
\TuningMainQuestions{} questions.
On the held-out side we removed the
\AccountingRemovedOwnHindiTrack{} Hindi documents (no gold source), the
\AccountingRemovedUniDocBench{} UniDoc-Bench documents (page-level gold
only) and the \AccountingRemovedQASPER{} QASPER documents (same rule),
which leaves \AccountingMain{} documents with \RQoneQuestions{}
questions. A question is dropped for a parser when its gold span cannot
be located in that parser's output; drop rates are in
\S\ref{sec:coverage}.

\subsection{Parsers and chunkers}
\label{sec:setups}

\paragraph{Parsers.} We compare Docling \DoclingVersion{}
\citep{auer2024docling} and pdfplumber \PdfplumberVersion{}\footnote{\url{https://github.com/jsvine/pdfplumber}}. Docling runs with its default pipeline (automatic OCR and
table-structure recognition; exact settings in Appendix~\ref{app:setups}); pdfplumber uses its default
text extraction, which reads no text from scanned pages.

\paragraph{Chunkers.} Every chunk length is counted with the bge-m3
tokenizer \citep{chen2024m3}, so all chunkers share one ruler. The pool contains fixed-size
token windows, recursive splitting with the separators of
\citet{demourajunior2026adaptive}, LangChain's recursive splitter,
sentence windows, page chunks, embedding-based semantic chunking,
LLM-proposed split patterns, and our own tree-based method
(Method~1). Method~1 builds a tree of sections, sub-sections, blocks and
sentences, from the parser's structure labels or, without them, from the
text's own numbering; a node becomes one chunk when its whole subtree
fits the target size, otherwise the chunker goes one level down and
merges small neighbouring siblings, so it never cuts inside a sentence,
list item or table row. Table chunks repeat the table's header row;
cross-references, planned in the design, are not part of the evaluated
version. Method~1 can add a
heading-path prefix to each chunk, capped at \PrefixCap{} of the chunk
size together with any table header. The size-based chunkers share a grid from
\SizeMin{} to \SizeMax{} tokens in steps of \SizeStep{}; the full grid, including overlap variants
and sentence windows, is in Appendix~\ref{app:setups}. After
chunking, every chunk above \ChunkMax{} tokens is split again and every
chunk under \ChunkMin{} tokens is merged into the preceding chunk when the result
stays within \ChunkMax{} tokens.

Across both parsers this gives \TuningSetups{} setups on the tuning set
and \HeldOutSetups{} on the held-out set, where the semantic chunker ran
out of memory. RQ1's primary analysis uses a subset of \RQoneSetups{}
setups, fixed in the held-out plan before the held-out set was opened:
recursive, LangChain recursive, fixed-size and Method~1 without prefix at
\RQonePrimarySizes{} tokens with no overlap, plus sentence windows of
\RQonePrimarySentenceWindow{} sentences, giving \RQonePrimarySettings{}
settings per parser (bold in Appendix~\ref{app:setups}). The plan gives
its reasons from the tuning results: sizes above these were worse for
every method, the prefix gave no measurable gain once its cap was fixed,
overlap gave no gain at the largest of these sizes, and the semantic and LLM-pattern chunkers ranked
lowest. Two sanity chunkers,
a random cut and a table-splitting cut, test that the scorer punishes
damage; they are never candidates. Short setup names are listed in
Appendix~\ref{app:key}.

\subsection{Retrieval and scoring}
\label{sec:scoring}

Each setup is indexed for hybrid retrieval: BM25 ($k_1=\BMKone{}$,
$b=\BMb{}$) and dense retrieval with bge-m3 (\EmbedDim{} dimensions,
cosine similarity), combined by reciprocal rank fusion with
$k=\RRFk{}$. No reranker is used.

Our main metric is \textbf{Recall@B}: chunks are read in rank order
until $B$ tokens have been read, and Recall@B is the share of gold-span
characters (ignoring whitespace) contained in that text. Prefix tokens
count against the budget, overlapping text is counted once, and the last
chunk is cut at $B$. The main budget is $B=\BudgetMain{}$, with
\BudgetLow{} and \BudgetHigh{} as checks. Because the budget is in
tokens, a setup with small chunks reads more chunks than one with large
chunks, so chunk size and retrieval depth are compared at equal reading
cost.

For RQ3 we also report the usual fixed-$k$ measures, which ignore chunk
length: Recall@\TopKSmall{} and Recall@\TopKLarge{} (gold characters in
the top $k$ chunks), Hit@1, MRR, and Recall at a \FixedKBudget{}-token
budget. The scorer is frozen as \texttt{scorer\_v1}; its rules exist
only as tagged code, which is therefore the specification
(Appendix~\ref{app:scorer}).

\subsection{Statistics}
\label{sec:stats}

Unless stated otherwise, intervals are \CILevel{}\% paired bootstrap intervals with
\Resamples{} resamples, clustered by document, so that documents with
many questions do not dominate. Proportions from the gold-label check use Wilson intervals.
Two setups \emph{tie} when the interval of their paired per-document
difference contains zero. The \emph{best single setup} is the one with
the highest mean among setups that are not clearly worse than the top setup;
cost does not break ties.

\paragraph{Per-document oracle (RQ1).} Choosing the best setup per
document and scoring it on the same questions rewards luck. We
therefore split each document's questions at random into two halves,
choose the best setup on one half and score it on the other, and repeat
this \SplitHalfReps{} times. Documents with a single question cannot be split; for them the oracle equals the
best single setup.
The \emph{gap} is the mean over documents of the oracle's score minus
the best single setup's score. RQ1 is GO only if the lower end of the
gap's interval is above zero; this rule was frozen before the held-out
set was scored. Where several comparisons are reported together, $p$
values are Holm-corrected.

\subsection{Answer quality (RQ4)}
\label{sec:aq}

For each document we sample \AQSampleShare{} of its questions (rounded up, at least one), drawn from questions
with a certain gold span located by both parsers; for scanned documents, which pdfplumber cannot read, the pool
is all located questions
(\AQTuningQuestions{} tuning, \AQHeldOutQuestions{} held-out and
\AQScannedQuestions{} scanned questions). A generator model
(gemini-3.5-flash-lite, temperature zero) answers from the top
retrieved chunks up to the main budget, or replies that the answer is
not found. The primary measures are token F1 and whether the answer
contains the collection's reference answer (any listed alternative is accepted). An LLM judge (Qwen3-8B) rates each answer as
fully, partly or not correct. Against a human on \JudgeCases{} cases it
agreed \ManualJudgeFirstPassAgreement{}\% at the first pass, below our
pre-set threshold, so we report the judge as a secondary measure only;
the check and its provenance are in Appendix~\ref{app:judge}.

\subsection{Deviations}
\label{sec:deviations}

We record \DeviationCount{} deviations from the pre-registered plan and \CorrectionCount{} later corrections,
each with its date (Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}). The most important
are these:
(i)~the held-out set was scored for three evaluations (RQ1, RQ5 and RQ4), and the RQ5 and RQ4 analyses were
specified after the RQ1 scores existed; held-out scores were also re-read, without new scoring, for descriptive
analyses;
(ii)~RQ5 and an extension of the chunker grid were added after the
tuning results were seen, but before the held-out set was scored;
(iii)~the RQ1 analysis rules were amended after the tuning run, before
the held-out set was scored;
(iv)~the answer judge was replaced and then failed its human check;
(v)~a check of \GoldenSampled{} questions from the Indian regulatory documents, each judged with its answer and
evidence, found \GoldenInvalid{} invalid and \GoldenUnsure{} unsure, and was stopped after its first batch; and
(vi)~the fixed-$k$ analysis first included superseded settings, which
a dated erratum corrects (Appendix~\ref{app:erratum}).
