\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation over PDFs depends on how each document is
parsed and cut into chunks, and a common proposal is to choose the parser and
chunker per document. We test whether this choice pays off in a study whose
design, scorer and decision rule were fixed before held-out data were scored.
We score \RQoneSetups{} parse-and-chunk setups (two parsers,
\RQonePrimarySettings{} chunker settings) on two sealed held-out sets,
\RQoneDocuments{} PDFs with \RQoneQuestions{} span-level questions and
\RQoneHTwoDropRuleDocuments{} ViDoRe V3 PDFs with
\RQoneHTwoDropRuleQuestions{} box-level questions, reading ranked chunks up to
a fixed budget of 1,250 tokens. A per-document oracle, cross-fitted on half of
each document's questions, falls below the best single setup on both sets
(gap \RQonePrimaryGap{} \RQonePrimaryGapCI{} and \RQoneHTwoDropRuleGap{}
\RQoneHTwoDropRuleGapCI{}), and the pre-registered rule gives NO-GO on both.
We find no evidence of a gain from choosing a setup per document, although
few questions per document limit what any chooser can learn. At a budget of
750 or 1,250 tokens, smaller chunks tend to retrieve more; under a fixed
number of chunks the ranking reverses, because large chunks put more text in
front of the reader. Tuning helps where a default is far off: tuned sizes beat
LangChain's default chunk size by \RQfiveBLangchainRecursivePdfplumberDiff{}
to \RQfiveBLangchainRecursiveDoclingDiff{} in recall, while parser settings
show no detectable gain. We release the code, the locked results and a tool
with fixed defaults.
\end{abstract}
