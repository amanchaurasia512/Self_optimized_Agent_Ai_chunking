\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether choosing a parse-and-chunk setup per PDF improves retrieval
over one fixed setup. On two sealed held-out sets, at 1,250 tokens and
under a decision rule written in advance, a cross-fitted per-document oracle
did not beat the best single setup, and we find no evidence of a gain from
per-document choice. This is not a bound on every chooser. Small chunks tend to
retrieve best at a budget of 750 or 1,250 tokens, which is not established at
2,000; on tuning data the ranking reverses when the number of chunks is fixed
instead; and in a post-hoc comparison, tuning helps against LangChain's default
chunk size. We release the code, the checksummed results and a tool with fixed defaults, so that these findings can be
checked and extended.
