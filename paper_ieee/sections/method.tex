\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

We ask one question first: how much retrieval quality is lost when a single
parse-and-chunk setup is used for every PDF, and how much of that loss an
oracle could recover by choosing a setup per document. To answer it fairly, we
fixed the design before looking at held-out data, placed gold evidence with one
rule for every parser, and compared setups by how much text they let the reader
see rather than by how many chunks they return. This section describes each
part in turn.


\subsection{Pre-registration}\label{sec:prereg}

We fixed the design, the scorer and the decision rule before any held-out score
existed, so that held-out results could not shape the rules used to judge them.
The decisions were kept in versioned specifications: v1 on 26~October 2026,
v1.1 on 1~November (chunker pool, oracle and cost) and v1.2 from 3~November
(held-out sets, scorer and analysis rules). The scorer was frozen on
5~November, and held-out set~1 was scored once, on 6~November.

A second held-out set was added later as a replication. Its document
selection, gold rule, one gold amendment and analysis code were written into
dated notes before it was scored, and it was also scored once, on
24~November 2026. Commit and first-push times for each step are given in
Table~\ref{tab:timeline}. Where we departed from the specification we say so:
Table~\ref{tab:deviations} lists \DeviationCount{} deviations and \CorrectionCount{} corrections.

\subsection{Documents and questions}\label{sec:docs}

We use three document sets, kept apart throughout.

\textbf{Tuning.} \TuningDocuments{} documents, \TuningMainDocuments{} of them with questions (\TuningMainQuestions{} in total), drawn
from CUAD contracts~\cite{hendrycks2021cuad}, OHR-Bench~\cite{zhang2024ocr}, an Indian
regulatory benchmark~\cite{singh2026parser}, QASPER~\cite{dasigi2021qasper} and
UniDoc-Bench~\cite{peng2025unidoc}. All design choices were made on this set.

\textbf{Held-out set 1.} \AccountingHeldOut{} sealed documents: unseen documents from the known
sources, one new document for each known document type, and out-of-domain
documents, including FinanceBench~\cite{islam2023financebench}. Some cannot be scored:
the Hindi pair has no question source, UniDoc documents have only page-level
gold, and some documents have only QASPER questions. This leaves \AccountingMain{} documents
with \RQoneQuestions{} questions.

\textbf{Held-out set 2.} To test whether the first result replicates on a
source the study had never used, we took the public English HR, Industrial and
Pharmaceuticals corpora of ViDoRe~V3~\cite{loison2026vidore}. Documents were selected
from metadata alone, before any was opened. We kept queries whose evidence
pages are all text or table pages within one document, and documents with at
least two such queries and at most 200 pages. This gives \HTwoSelectedDocuments{} documents % src: spec/spec_v1.2.md:1327-1328
(\HTwoSelectedHr{} HR, \HTwoSelectedIndustrial{} Industrial, \HTwoSelectedPharmaceuticals{} Pharmaceuticals) and 286 queries, none overlapping % src: spec/spec_v1.2.md:1328
the other sets. Under the primary gold rule (Section~\ref{sec:gold}),
\RQoneHTwoDropRuleDocuments{} documents and \RQoneHTwoDropRuleQuestions{} questions can be scored.

Scanned PDFs for RQ5(a) come from MP-DocVQA~\cite{tito2023mpdocvqa}.

\subsection{Gold}\label{sec:gold}

Each question needs its evidence located in the text a parser produces. One
matching rule places the gold evidence as character spans in each parser's
own output. For held-out set~1 the evidence is the answer string given by each
source; if the rule cannot place it with certainty, the question is dropped.
This removes \CoverageHeldOutDoclingShare{} of held-out set~1 questions for Docling and \CoverageHeldOutPdfplumberShare{} for
pdfplumber.

For held-out set~2 the evidence is the words inside the human-drawn evidence
boxes, read from the PDF text layer. Under the primary rule, a page is kept
when the matcher's frozen drop rule (threshold \GoldDropThreshold{}) accepts every piece of
its box text. Two variants serve as sensitivity checks: one keeps a query if
any of its evidence pages is placed, and a stricter certain-only variant
applies the held-out set~1 rule. Certain-only was designed for short answer
strings. On box text it drops pages because of how they are matched rather
than what they contain, and it keeps far more questions for pdfplumber than
for Docling. We therefore moved it to a pre-stated sensitivity check on
23~November, before any held-out set~2 score.

All comparisons use only the questions kept for every setup being compared.
This keeps the comparison fair, but a parser that places more gold, as Docling
does, gets no credit for it in recall. We report the drop rates above so that
this coverage difference stays visible. In held-out set~2 the gold comes from
the boxes and does not depend on the parser at the source, but it still has
to be placed in each parser's text.

\subsection{Setups}\label{sec:setups}

A setup is one parser combined with one chunker setting. The parsers are
Docling~2.143.0~\cite{auer2024docling} with default settings (Table~\ref{tab:docling})
and pdfplumber~0.11.10. The chunkers (Table~\ref{tab:chunkers}) are
fixed-token, recursive, LangChain recursive (including its default settings),
sentence groups, semantic, LLM-regex and page-based chunking, plus our own
structure-tree method, M1. Every chunker shares one final step: chunks shorter
than 100 tokens are merged, and chunks longer than 1,024 tokens are split
again.

M1 builds a tree from the blocks the parser returns. A node that fits within
the target size $T$ is kept whole; one that does not is opened, and its
children are handled the same way. Neighbouring pieces under the same parent
are packed together, and a block that is still too large is cut between
sentences or, for tables, between rows, with the table header repeated in
each piece. Optionally, each chunk carries a prefix with its ancestors'
labels, limited to 10\% of $T$.

The primary choice set was fixed before held-out set~1 was opened: recursive,
LangChain recursive, fixed-token and M1 without prefix, each at 256, 384 and
512 tokens, plus sentence groups of five. That gives \RQonePrimarySettings{} settings and, with two
parsers, \RQoneSetups{} setups. Spec~v1.1 also planned paragraph groups, dynamic token
size, section- and table-aware chunking and Docling's HybridChunker. These were
built later and tested on tuning data only, as an extension
(Section~\ref{sec:extension}). A cross-reference feature planned for M1 was
dropped and never built.

\subsection{Retrieval and scoring}\label{sec:scoring}

Each setup is scored with three retrievers: BM25~\cite{bm25} ($k_1=1.5$,
$b=0.75$), dense retrieval with bge-m3~\cite{chen2024m3} (1,024 dimensions), and a
hybrid that combines the two by reciprocal rank fusion~\cite{rrf} with $k=60$.

Our main measure is Recall@$B$. We read the ranked chunks in order until $B$
tokens have been used and count the share of gold characters (ignoring
whitespace) that appear in the text read. Prefix tokens count toward the
budget, text already read is counted once, and the last chunk is cut at the
budget. Fixing $B$ means every setup shows the reader the same amount of text,
so setups are compared on what they retrieve, not on how much. We use
$B \in \{750, 1{,}250, 2{,}000\}$; the headline result is hybrid
Recall@1,250.

Before freezing the scorer, we checked that it responds to chunk quality:
chunks cut at random positions score \SanityFloorLower{} lower, and splitting every
table lowers recall by \SanityDamageDrop{} on \SanityDamageQuestions{} table questions. As a robustness
check named in spec~v1, we repeated the analysis on tuning data with
nomic-embed-text-v1.5~\cite{nomic} as the dense embedder.

\subsection{Statistics}\label{sec:stats}

The document is the unit of analysis. Question scores are averaged within each
document, and confidence intervals come from a bootstrap over
documents~\cite{bootstrap} (2,000 resamples, 95\% level, seed 42). Two setups
are tied when the paired interval of their per-document difference contains
zero. The best single setup is the one with the highest mean, and cost does
not break ties.

This tie rule changed during the study. Specs~v1 and v1.1 said that the
cheaper setup wins a tie and defined the oracle as the cheapest tied setup. A
development run on tuning data showed two problems with this. First, setups
without overlap index almost the same text, so their costs differ by about
0.1 token per document, and the cheapest tied setup was chosen by noise: the % src: spec/spec_v1.2.md:140-143
best single setup became pdfplumber with groups of eight sentences (\CheapestTieBestRecall{}),
while the top setup scored \TuningTopRecall{}. Second, per-document tie tests have almost
no power on documents with few questions (98 of \TuningSetups{} setups tied at four % src: spec/spec_v1.2.md:140-143
questions), so the cheapest-tied oracle fell below the best single setup on
8 documents. An amendment on 5~November, the day before held-out set~1 was % src: spec/spec_v1.2.md:140-143
scored, therefore made the rule recall-only and introduced the split-half
oracle described below. Cost is reported separately, on two Pareto fronts,
index tokens and chunk count (Table~\ref{tab:cost}).

An oracle that picks each document's best setup using the same questions it is
scored on would gain from chance alone. We avoid this with split-half
cross-fitting: over 200 random splits, the oracle picks the best setup on one
half of a document's questions and is measured on the other half. The gap is
the mean over documents of the oracle's recall minus that of the best single
setup. The decision rule was fixed before held-out set~1 was opened: a
per-document chooser is worth building only if the lower end of the gap's
interval is above zero. Multiple comparisons use Holm's correction~\cite{holm}.

For held-out set~2, the following were fixed before scoring: the same rule for
each gold variant; an extra check that leaves out the questions on \RQoneHRCheckPages{} HR pages
whose image and PDF shapes differ by more than 5\%; and, as a secondary
result, a gap pooled over both held-out sets. The pooled gap compares the
split-half oracle with the tuning winner applied unchanged, weights documents
equally and uses a bootstrap stratified by set. It measures a different
quantity from the per-set gap, and the two are never combined.

RQ2 was first examined on tuning data as a descriptive analysis, added on
12~November after the tuning scores were known: for each parser, budget and
source, Spearman's $\rho$ between measured chunk size and recall over all
in-scope settings, without tests. A confirmatory rule was fixed before
held-out set~2 was scored: for each parser and budget, Spearman's $\rho$ over
that parser's \RQtwoHTwoSetupsPerParser{} primary setups, with a
document-bootstrap interval; RQ2 holds if every $\rho$ is negative.

\subsection{Answer quality}\label{sec:aq}

Recall@$B$ shows whether the gold text is read, not whether a language model
can answer from it. To check that answers rank setups the way recall does, we
fixed a separate design on 10~November 2026, before running it. It uses
held-out set~1 a third time, for this question only, and we disclose it as
such.

From each PDF we sample \AQSampleShare{} of its questions (rounded up, at least one;
seed 42). On tuning and held-out documents, the pool is the questions with
certain gold spans shared by both parsers. For scanned PDFs it is all \ScannedQuestions{}
MP-DocVQA questions, since no located gold is needed. On digital PDFs we
compare seven Docling setups: M1 at 256 and 384 tokens, LangChain recursive at
256 and 512, recursive at 512, fixed-token at 1,024 and LangChain's default,
all without prefix or overlap. On scanned PDFs we compare the five RQ5
settings with Docling's default OCR against the same settings with Tesseract
OCR.

The context for each question is exactly what the scorer reads: hybrid
retrieval, with chunks in rank order up to 1,250 tokens. Gemini~3.5 Flash-Lite
answers at temperature \AnswerTemperature{} with one fixed prompt that asks it to answer only
from the context or reply ``not found''. Each answer is compared with the
source's own reference answer. The primary measures are token F1 and whether
the reference answer is contained in the response. An LLM judge gives a
secondary score; an amendment on 11~November changed it from Gemini to an
open-weight model, Qwen3-8B (4-bit, greedy decoding). Setups are compared with
the same paired document bootstrap and Holm correction as above, and we report
Spearman's $\rho$ between F1 and Recall@1,250 across setups.
