The result of RQ2 depends on fixing the number of tokens read. When the number of chunks is fixed instead, the ranking reverses: on both
parsers, Recall@10 rises with chunk size while Recall@1,250 falls

\RQthreeSettingsLeftOut{} superseded settings are left out
(Table~\ref{tab:rq3err}), Recall@5 is highest for
\RQthreeDoclingRecallFiveBest{} on both parsers (measured size
\RQthreeDoclingRecallFiveSize{} and \RQthreePdfplumberRecallFiveSize{}
tokens), and Recall@10 for \RQthreeDoclingRecallOneZeroBest{}
(\RQthreeDoclingRecallOneZeroSize{} and \RQthreePdfplumberRecallOneZeroSize{}

intervals or tests. MRR is nominally highest for \RQthreeDoclingMrrBest{}
(\RQthreeDoclingMrrSize{} tokens) on Docling and \RQthreePdfplumberMrrBest{}
(\RQthreePdfplumberMrrSize{} tokens) on pdfplumber. Hit@1 is nominally highest for a large setting on pdfplumber (\RQthreePdfplumberHitOneBest{},
\RQthreePdfplumberHitOneSize{} tokens) but a small one on Docling
(\RQthreeDoclingHitOneBest{}, \RQthreeDoclingHitOneSize{} tokens).
Under a token budget, small chunks score higher instead, subject to the limits
stated for RQ2.

The control on tuning data (\RQthreeSweepDocuments{} documents) supports a reason.
Over four chunker families and two parsers, size 256 minus size 1,024 is
\RQthreeSweepBudget{} in Recall@1,250 but \RQthreeSweepRecall{} in Recall@5; in
Lift@5, where the recall of a random chunk order is subtracted, it is
\RQthreeSweepLift{}. All \RQthreeSweepSignificant{} of the \RQthreeSweepTests{}
tested differences are significant (largest Holm $p$ =
\RQthreeSweepMaxHolm{}), and no family reaches a crossover up to
\FixedKBudget{} tokens. Under a fixed number of chunks, a large chunk puts more
text in front of the reader, so Recall@$k$ rewards size itself. A fixed token
budget removes that advantage, and any comparison of chunk sizes has to say
which of the two it uses.

\subsection{The released tool}\label{sec:tool}

Following the NO-GO verdict, the tool does not choose a setup per document. Its
default mode applies the setup chosen on tuning data, \RQoneTuningWinner{}, to
every PDF; scan detection selects nothing there, because Docling is always used.
Its fast mode keeps the chunker but parses with pdfplumber, unless a page has no
text layer; then the whole document goes to Docling. Fast mode on digital PDFs
is therefore not the tuned setup.
