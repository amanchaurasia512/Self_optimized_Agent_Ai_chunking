The result of RQ2 depends on fixing the number of tokens read. When the number of chunks is fixed instead, the ranking reverses: on both
parsers, Recall@10 rises with chunk size while Recall@1,250 falls
(Fig.~\ref{fig:rq3}). On
tuning data, over the \RQthreeSettingsKept{} settings that remain after
\RQthreeSettingsLeftOut{} superseded settings are left out
(Table~\ref{tab:rq3err}), Recall@5 is highest for
\RQthreeDoclingRecallFiveBest{} on both parsers (measured size
\RQthreeDoclingRecallFiveSize{} and \RQthreePdfplumberRecallFiveSize{}
tokens), and Recall@10 for \RQthreeDoclingRecallOneZeroBest{}
(\RQthreeDoclingRecallOneZeroSize{} and \RQthreePdfplumberRecallOneZeroSize{}
tokens). MRR also favours large chunks: \RQthreeDoclingMrrBest{}
(\RQthreeDoclingMrrSize{} tokens) on Docling and \RQthreePdfplumberMrrBest{}
(\RQthreePdfplumberMrrSize{} tokens) on pdfplumber. Hit@1 is split: a large
setting wins on pdfplumber (\RQthreePdfplumberHitOneBest{},
\RQthreePdfplumberHitOneSize{} tokens) but a small one on Docling
(\RQthreeDoclingHitOneBest{}, \RQthreeDoclingHitOneSize{} tokens).
Under a token budget, small chunks win instead, as RQ2 shows for every budget
up to 2,000 tokens.

Under a fixed number of chunks, a large chunk puts more text in front of the
reader, so measures such as Recall@$k$ and MRR reward size itself. A fixed
token budget removes that advantage, and any comparison of chunk sizes has to
say which of the two it uses.
